## KEY POINTS

### 1. REFERENCE VIDEO = YOUR GUIDE
- Watch the reference video multiple times before starting
- Match the visual style: 3D rendered / stop-motion Barbie dolls, pastel color palette, soft lighting, miniature sets
- Match the pacing and emotional arc: problem → struggle → discovery → transformation → confidence
- You decide how to build the scenes — the reference shows you the level of quality and detail we expect

### 2. SCENES MUST MATCH THE VOICE-OVER
- This is the most important rule: what the viewer HEARS must match what they SEE
- When the voice-over talks about hair falling out in the shower → show a bathroom scene
- When the voice-over talks about the doctor → show a doctor scene
- When the voice-over mentions the product → show the product
- Never show a scene that contradicts or has nothing to do with the current voice-over line
- Read the full script before you start so you understand the story flow

### 3. AUDIO MUST BE CLEAN
- Voice-over: clear, studio-quality recording. No echo, no background noise, no compression artifacts
- Multiple characters speak in the script (Barbie, a friend, a doctor, a colleague) — use distinct voices so the viewer can tell who is talking
- Barbie's voice should be emotional/vulnerable in the beginning and confident by the end
- Pacing: conversational and natural, not rushed. Let emotional moments breathe — don't speed through the crying or the transformation moments

### 4. MUSIC MUST FIT THE MOOD
- The music should support the emotional arc of the story, not overpower it
- Beginning (problem/struggle): soft, emotional — piano or acoustic
- Middle (discovery/hope): gradually more uplifting
- End (transformation/confidence): warm, positive, empowering
- Music volume must ALWAYS sit underneath the voice-over — the dialogue must be clearly audible at all times
- Reference: think Pixar emotional moments — subtle, supportive, never distracting

### 5. HAIR PROGRESSION
- Barbie's hair tells the visual story of the entire ad
- Beginning: hair is THIN, wider part line, sparse — she looks like she's losing her hair
- After she finds the product: hair starts recovering, baby hairs visible at the hairline
- End: hair is FULL, thick, shiny — she wears it open and down with confidence
- This progression must be visually obvious — the viewer should clearly see the difference between beginning and end

### 6. PRODUCT PACKAGING
- The product in the video must be **HaarVita by NATURAPlus™**
- Use ONLY the product packaging image we provide — do NOT design a new one
- The packaging must look exactly like our real product: same colors, same text, same layout
- When the product appears on screen, it must be clearly visible and readable

### 7. SUBTITLES
- White text, subtle drop shadow, centered in the bottom third
- Synced word-by-word to the voice-over (same style as the reference video)
- Clean, readable font
- All subtitles in German — no English anywhere

### 8. NO ENGLISH
- Everything in the video must be in German: subtitles, any on-screen text, product labels
- The voice-over script is provided in German — do not translate or change it
- German umlauts (ä, ö, ü, ß) must display correctly

### 9. OUTFIT CHANGES = TIME PASSING
- In the reference video, Barbie's outfit changes to show that time has passed (gray outfit when struggling → pink pajamas during recovery → power outfit at the end)
- Use the same concept: outfit changes signal a new chapter in the story

### 10. THE PRODUCT REVEAL MOMENT
- When Barbie shows the product to someone, this is the key moment of the ad
- Make it feel natural — she pulls it out of her bag, shows it to a friend/colleague
- The product must be in focus, well-lit, clearly readable
- Don't rush this scene
